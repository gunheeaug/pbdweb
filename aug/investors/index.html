It's a Spatial Social

Yes, but aug is not just another social platform - it's spatial. It pulls users out of the 2D scroll feed and into the real-world camera scene.

You can instantly create and share AR text, photos, GIF stickers, and more spatial content for others nearby to discover and interact with. Creating spatial content should feel as easy and native as posting an IG Story or TikTok.

Why aug -
The Three Shifts

  1. 1. Consumer AR as a data funnel for Physical AI

    Aug naturally incentivizes users to generate valuable video, movement, and spatial data for AI. Data captured through mobile AR using LiDAR, depth sensing, and spatial mapping can contain structured information about real-world geometry, objects, surfaces, distances, and movement—not just pixels from a regular mobile video.

    We’ve already seen the strategic value of large-scale geospatial and real-world datasets. The opportunity is to build consumer AR people genuinely want to use, while generating high-quality spatial data as part of the experience.

  2. 2. Spatial content pulls users toward glasses

    People will purchase their first pair of smart glasses when there is content, utility, or a social experience compelling enough to pull them there. Aug builds user-generated spatial content and communities on mobile first, then can leverage that existing network to give users a reason to move onto glasses and experience that content in a more native form.

  3. 3. AR needs a UGC ecosystem

    We do not need to wait for better glasses hardware for AR to become meaningful. Pokémon GO already proved the potential of mobile AR at massive scale. The missing piece has been user-generated content.

    While Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, and YouTube grew through creator ecosystems, AR has largely remained publisher-driven. Aug changes that by making AR creation instant, social, and user-driven—thoughts, reactions, selfies, memes, and what is happening in the moment, shared in seconds through the camera.

Problems We Solve

  • Social is Stuck in the Limited 2D Feed Real Estate

    Attention lives in scroll timelines, while real life happens in places. There’s no native, easy way to leave and discover social content in the camera scene.

  • Mobile AR has been publisher-driven

    Most AR experiences ship a single content universe from a single provider. Without a creator flywheel, spatial experiences do not compound the way social apps do.

  • Creation friction kills spatial User Generated Content (UGC)

    Studio-grade 3D is not what most people want to post. If creating AR content isn’t as easy as a Story or TikTok, the ecosystem never forms. In fact, Apple's RealityKit framework does not include a direct, built-in API to record the rendered 3D scene into a video file, so we had to develop our own.

Go-to-Market

Wedge: K-pop and Anime Fandoms

We start by turning meaningful fan locations—concert venues, pop-ups, event spaces, outdoor billboards, and other places tied to a favorite idol or character—into shared augmented spaces.

Fans open Aug at these locations and interact through a persistent augmented board, leaving supportive messages, posting reactions, sharing stickers and fan-created content, and discovering what other fans have left behind.

Then Expand

Fandom locations → broader place-based social communities → a wider spatial content network, including Out-of-Home (OOH) and brand moments as the network grows.
